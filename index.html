About Me

Hi, my name is Jitesh. I am currently a Junior Research Fellow (JRF) at Deparment of physics at IISER Bhopal, India. I completed my BS-MS Dual degree with major in physic in 2025. My broad research interest lies in theoretical high-energy physics and quantum field theory, with a focus on the formulation and consistency of field theories in perturbative and non-perturbative regimes. I am also interested in effective field theories, EFT bootstrap, black hole physics, holography, and the applications of field-theoretic methods in condensed matter systems.

I come from Bihar, a state in eastern India known for its rich history and deep philosophical traditions. When I’m not working on a physics problem, I enjoy reading philosophical books, noticing things around me, and playing basketball and table tennis. I’m also fascinated by how ideas evolve—whether in science, society, or individual minds.

Research

  • Pathology in quantum field theories: In the formulation of field theory, and in general relativity, we demand hyperbolic evolution of the system for well-posedness. However, self-interactions may alter the evolution and render the system ill-posed. In my thesis, I focused on massive self-interacting vector fields with mass added by hand and utilized the hyperbolic and non-covariant formulation to analyze their behavior in the non-perturbative regime and show how the ill-posedness at classical level (At the level of equation of motion) give rise to unitarity voilation at quantum level.

    Master's Thesis: Self-intracting massive vector fields and its massless limit

    • Preprint: arXiv:2505.22119

    Abstract: We study massive self-interacting vector field theories with mass added ``by hand". We show that the massless limit of the quartic self-interacting vector field theory is not smooth. Pathological behavior of the theory is not limited only at the classical level, even at the quantum level unitarity is violated at the two-loop. Using the Vainshtein mechanism, we show that it fails beyond the strong-coupling scale and hence a massless limit is not smooth even in quantum theory.

Previous projects

  • High-Harmonic Generation in Confined Hydrogen Atoms: This project focuses on obtaining accurate results for hydrogen-like atoms under intense external fields. We employ a non-perturbative framework to simulate the generation of high-order harmonics induced by ultrashort, intense pulsed laser fields. Our numerical implementation is based on the Split Operator Method combined with a generalized pseudospectral approach, which effectively handles the Coulomb singularity and semi-infinite domain problems. This method was originally developed by Shih-I Chu et al. (1996). We uses this technique to predict the high-harmonic generation (HHG) spectra of endohedrally confined hydrogen atoms — specifically, hydrogen atoms trapped within C60 cages — and compared these results with the spectra of free (unconfined) hydrogen atoms.
  • Dessins d’Enfants and Galois Orbit Prediction: This was my first experience working with abstract mathematical structures like dessins d’enfants. I began by studying foundational topics including Riemann surfaces, Belyi maps, dessins, and Galois actions. I then explored a dataset of dessins in multiple representations — namely, the matrix form and the cyclic edge list form. My analysis started with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to estimate the intrinsic (true) dimension of the dataset. Finally, I trained a neural network (a multilayer perceptron) to predict the Galois orbit size of each dessin.
